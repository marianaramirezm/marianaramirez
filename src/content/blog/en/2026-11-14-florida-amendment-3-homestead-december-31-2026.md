Florida voters approved **Amendment 3 (House Joint Resolution 1-F)** on November 3, 2026. Department of Revenue bulletin **PTO 26-21** (November 5, 2026) says it takes effect **January 1, 2027** and first applies to the **2027 tax roll**.

If you are shopping Miami or anywhere in Florida and keep hearing about a December 31 deadline, here is the clear version: what matters is **permanent Florida residency**, not the closing date.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Confirm with the Miami-Dade Property Appraiser and a CPA or attorney before you decide.

### What Amendment 3 changes

Per PTO 26-21 and the enrolled text of CS/HJR 1-F, Amendment 3 does three things buyers ask about most:

1. **Raises the homestead exemption for non-school taxes** to assessed value of up to **$150,000 in 2027** and up to **$250,000 in 2028**, then adjusts for inflation.
2. **Keeps the school-district homestead exemption at $25,000.** School taxes are not eliminated.
3. **Lowers the annual assessment-increase cap on non-homestead property** from **10% to 5%** beginning January 1, 2027, for non-school taxes only. That covers second homes, investment properties, and rentals without homestead.

The Department also says **rules, forms, and guidance are still coming**. Use these figures as the constitutional framework and watch for local administration updates.

### Homestead exemption amounts: before and after

Exemptions reduce **assessed value**. They are not a fixed dollar savings figure. Your bill still depends on taxable value, millage rates, and non-ad valorem assessments.

For **2026**, DOR brochure **PT-113** still controls: **$25,000** applied to all property taxes (including school), plus an **additional non-school exemption** adjusted for inflation when CPI is positive. The Volusia County Property Appraiser’s Amendment 3 FAQ, citing DOR CPI certification, lists that additional 2026 amount as **$26,411**, for a total of up to **$51,411**.

| Tax year | School homestead | Non-school homestead (owners eligible for increased amounts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2026** (current) | $25,000 | Additional CPI amount ($26,411 per Volusia FAQ), for up to about $51,411 total |
| **2027** | $25,000 | Up to $150,000 of assessed value |
| **2028** | $25,000 | Up to $250,000 of assessed value; inflation adjustments follow |

### December 31, 2026: residency, not closing

The **operative text** of CS/HJR 1-F sets a different structure for someone who, on or after January 1, 2027, owns and maintains a permanent residence in Florida but **had not maintained a permanent residence in this state as of December 31, 2026**. Those owners receive:

- up to **$25,000** assessed value exempt for **school** levies; and
- up to **$50,000** assessed value exempt for **non-school** levies;

with the **increased** non-school exemption beginning in the **fifth year** of exemption under that provision. PTO 26-21 restates the same rule.

| | Permanent FL resident **as of Dec 31, 2026** | **Not** a permanent FL resident as of Dec 31, 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School homestead (when you qualify) | $25,000 | $25,000 |
| Non-school homestead (when you qualify) | Up to $150,000 in 2027; up to $250,000 in 2028+ | Up to $50,000 until the fifth year, then the increased amount |
| Must you buy before Dec 31? | No. Residency is the issue. You still must later own the home, make it your permanent residence, and file for homestead. | Closing early does not unlock the larger exemption if you were not a permanent Florida resident by Dec 31, 2026. |

Buying before December 31 does **not** automatically place you in the higher group. A permanent Florida resident by that date can buy later and seek the increased amounts once homestead is approved. Someone who becomes a Florida resident only afterward starts under the limited structure until year five.

Flag this mismatch: the **ballot summary** says a person who establishes Florida residency **“after January 1, 2027”** must maintain residency for five years before the increased exemption. The **operative text** uses **“as of December 31, 2026.”** Confirm with the **Miami-Dade Property Appraiser** how that cutoff will be administered once implementing guidance is final.

### How homestead still works

Amendment 3 changes the amounts. The basics in DOR **PT-113** remain:

- **Own** the property and make it your **permanent residence** (or a dependent’s) **as of January 1**.
- File Form **DR-501** with the county property appraiser by **March 1**.
- Only the property appraiser decides whether it qualifies.

Homestead is for a permanent Florida residence. Second homes and investment properties do not receive homestead benefits simply because Amendment 3 passed.

### Ownership changes and portability (existing rules)

From DOR **PT-112**, stated as they exist today, with **no claim** about Amendment 3 interacting with Save Our Homes or portability beyond the amendment’s own text:

- After a **change of ownership**, homestead is generally assessed at **just (market) value the following January 1**, unless an exception applies.
- **Save Our Homes portability** may transfer all or part of the assessment difference to a new Florida homestead if you establish it within **three years** of January 1 of the year you abandoned the prior homestead, and file the required forms (including **DR-501T**) by March 1.

For non-homestead owners, the assessment-increase cap for non-school taxes drops from 10% to 5% beginning January 1, 2027. School taxes on non-homestead property stay based on just/market value without that cap.

### Practical takeaways for Miami buyers

1. Document permanent Florida residency by December 31, 2026 if that timeline is real for you. If not, plan for the limited exemption until year five.
2. Keep school and non-school separate: $25,000 school stays; larger Amendment 3 figures are non-school.
3. Exemptions cut assessed value. They do not erase the bill.
4. Investors and second-home buyers may feel the cap dropping from 10% to 5% more than the homestead increase, unless they later establish a qualifying permanent residence.
5. Watch DOR and Miami-Dade updates on forms and filing rules.
